## Primary results

Scores use a forced-choice four-point scale. Differences are controlled minus comparator and aggregate four questions across two matched call contexts.

| Comparison | Controlled | Comparator | Difference (95% bootstrap CI) | Sign-flip p | Cohen's dz |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Controlled A4 vs raw AMT | 2.787 | 2.945 | -0.159 [-0.326, 0.006] | 0.0785 | -0.289 |
| Controlled A6 vs motif baseline | 1.909 | 2.256 | -0.348 [-0.534, -0.162] | 0.0009 | -0.570 |

Presentation-position adjustment produced similar effects: -0.164 for A4 vs raw and -0.349 for A6 vs motif. The first comparison remains inconclusive; the second favors the motif baseline on the selected stimuli.

## Interpretation boundary

The study used eight fallback-free fixed stimuli from four call contexts, one preset, pre-rendered piano audio, and 41 WeChat-recruited participants. It does not estimate the full output distributions of the systems. Across the eight items, objective score and human rating had little descriptive alignment (Pearson r = -0.054; Spearman rho = 0.071), but this correlation is exploratory because n = 8.

The evidence supports phrase-level control as a structural and engineering intervention. It does not support claiming that controlled AMT was preferred by listeners in this experiment.
